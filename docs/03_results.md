# Results

## Preprocessing and assembly

199,495 raw read pairs were reduced to:

- 198,528 pairs after putative PCR duplicate removal
- 188,573 pairs after quality control
- 188,573 nonhuman pairs after GRCh38 screening

Assembly produced 1,597 contigs ≥500 bp and 139 contigs ≥1 kb.

geNomad identified 54 viral contigs among the ≥1-kb assembly.

Eighteen contigs belonged to strict GastroCap target families.

## Reference-guided viral recovery

Competitive reference mapping showed strong support for several viral targets.

Three particularly strong reconstructions were:

- HAstV-1 population A
- HAstV-3
- Rotavirus A segment 3

HAstV-1 population A and HAstV-3 yielded near-complete consensus genomes.

## Distinct HAstV-1 populations

Two HAstV-1-associated assembled contigs showed reciprocal reference specificity.

`k141_843`:

- 99.08% identity to HAstV1_A reference
- 88.97% identity to HAstV1_B reference

`k141_1995`:

- 90.56% identity to HAstV1_A reference
- 99.64% identity to HAstV1_B reference

The two reconstructed HAstV-1 ORF2 sequences shared only 90.24% nucleotide identity across 1,997 callable positions.

Phylogenetic analysis placed both populations within HAstV-1 but in distinct public-sequence clusters.

## HAstV-3

The HAstV-3 consensus was callable across 99.96% of the reference genome with a mean depth of 255.6×.

ORF2 showed 99.75% identity to MN444721.1.

## Diagnostic mismatch surveillance

No primer 3'-terminal mismatches were detected in the evaluated public HAstV assays.

HAstV1_B retained complete sequence compatibility with the forward, reverse, and probe oligonucleotides of one evaluated classical HAstV assay despite substantial genome-level divergence from HAstV1_A.

The BCCDC HAstV probe showed two mismatches against the HAstV-3 consensus.

These findings illustrate that overall viral sequence divergence does not necessarily predict divergence at diagnostic oligonucleotide binding sites.
